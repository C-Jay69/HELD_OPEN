## 1. The four fixes you approved

**1a. Interlude III — the weekday.** *"The boy came up on **Thursday** at nine minutes past ten"* →
*"The boy came up on **Sunday**…"* The interlude is dated Day 104. Day 0 is 1 December 2025, which
is a Monday, so Day 104 is **Sunday 15 March 2026** — and the hospital ward in the scene has the
Sunday quiet on it. One word. No reader would ever have caught this; it was only visible on a
calendar, which is exactly why it was worth fixing.

**1b. Chapter Eighteen — "nine weeks to the day."** → *"**ten** weeks to the day."* The withdrawal
of the vessel is on **17 February**. Nine weeks to the day from 17 February is **21 April** — the
morning Elena interviews her father, the day the book's whole last movement turns on. Nothing in the
text referred to it, so the collision was invisible: but a sharp reader who counts would have found
the most important day in the book quietly duplicated by a throwaway phrase. Ten weeks lands on
7 April, clear of everything.

**1c. The twelve days stay in Chapter 17.** Your call, and no change was made. Chapter 17 reads
**Days 53–70** and keeps the twelve days that were added at the front of the block. Nothing moved.

**1d. Chapter Nineteen's day range.** The heading now reads **Days 83–85** instead of Days 83–84.
*"We have seventy-two hours"* is exact if the question is put on the Tuesday (Day 85, the day before
the rescue week), and the range now permits it. The overlap with Chapter 20's opening Day 85 is
deliberate: that meeting runs across the chapter break.

## 2. Two corrections to my own work in D9

**2a. Beat A's clock (end of Chapter Eleven).** I had Okafor leaving his desk *"at twenty past
nine"* for air. The queue closes at six and the rota runs after it; twenty past nine was my
invention, not his shift. He now takes his badge *"at twenty past six… which was what everybody did
at the end of the shift."* The beat also ends better: it used to finish on a closed count, which was
a small pre-emption of the interlude's later reflection. It now finishes on the thing that matters —
he is in the car park before it occurs to him that she did not answer the question.

**2b. Beat B's last line (end of Chapter Twenty-Two).** I wrote *"he did not write anything in the
book that day, **or for twenty-six days**."* The arithmetic was exact (Day 96 + 26 = Day 122 = Four
Doors) but it contradicted Interlude III, which has **thirty-one entries in the book nine days
later**. The line now reads: *"he did not write in the book again until the Monday, when he wrote
three lines in a row and stopped telling himself it was a list."* Same effect, no contradiction, and
it keeps the reader's eye on the handwriting rather than on a countdown.

## 3. Four openings moved from narration to voice

All forty-two sections of the book opened on narration. Four now open on the first line of speech,
with the narration that used to stand in front of it moved behind it, so that it reads as the second
beat instead of the first. **Not one word of dialogue was touched, nothing was cut or added, and no
scene changed shape.** The reader now meets the voice before the explanation — which is how a
reader should meet a deciding character.

| chapter | now opens on | the narration now in second position |
|---|---|---|
| **Ch 20 — The Price** | *"Two vehicles, one night, seventy-two hours, three people and a priest who will not lie for us. I am going to ask for something and I want to hear the refusals in the order people are willing to say them. Who goes."* | *"She put the question properly on the eighty-fifth day, standing up, with Father Ward's Friday on the table."* |
| **Ch 22 — Ransom, or Something Like It** | *"A man in a facility is not in a cell. He is in a schedule…"* | *"Roux said the thing that decided everything on the first morning, standing at the board with a marker in her good hand, without any drama at all."* |
| **Ch 30 — Casualties** | *"Four minutes each way," she said. "Eleven minutes unaccounted for, in a village of nine buildings, one of which is closed."* | *"By nine in the morning Roux had established that the green bag was not in the house…"* |
| **Ch 36 — The Unplugging** | *"There is a man in this house with a summons in his pocket, and the date on it is the sixth of May, and the room on it is ours."* | *"Mara took it to the room on the evening of the thirtieth, in the kitchen, standing up, because she had spent five months learning that a person who sits down to say a hard thing has already softened it."* |

The first two of these were the real wins: *Who goes* is a better first line for the chapter that
decides the rescue than a sentence about a table, and Roux's speech about a man in a schedule is the
thesis of the chapter that springs him.

Chapters Two and Three were considered and deliberately left alone: both have staged cold-opens that
are doing real work — *"nobody had replaced the toner"* and *"a paper map… which Mara Voss found
funny until she needed it"* — and those are not sentences to bury under dialogue.
