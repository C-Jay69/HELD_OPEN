## 1. What is new

**`Chapter Twenty-Three — The Log Man`** (Days 96–99, 1,790 words).

Inserted immediately after *Chapter Twenty-Two — Ransom, or Something Like It*, i.e. before
*CUSTOS Interlude III: Kestrel Field*.

It is the launch man from the Second Run: the man with the notebook and the biro who came
alongside at 03:20 on the night of the run and said he would log the starboard lamp. Mara told him
to log it. The manuscript's rule was that she never told the room — she told El Búho. This chapter
spends that.

Structure: the harbour-administration notice at Day 96 · Roux's objection (the doctrine forbids
this) against El Búho's answer (*"This is a debt"*) · the four-hour drive north and the nine rules
for a day like that · the car park at Killybegs · the four minutes between two men in the rain ·
the drive home · Sal's line about which things have no column · the April postscript, in which the
machine sorts the man without ever being told about him.

Nothing else in the manuscript was altered to accommodate it. The withdrawal of the vessel from
the coastal service in April already existed at the end of Chapter Eighteen; this chapter is the
cause of that sentence.

## 3. Continuity corrections made inside the new chapter before insertion

- **El Búho's age in 1988** — was "I was thirty-eight"; the manuscript's own arithmetic (he is 74
  now, Elena is 41, the 1992 raid is on the page with a seven-year-old) makes 2026 the present year,
  so in 1988 he was thirty-six. Corrected to **thirty-six**.
- **The lamp's date** — the run's night is the sixteenth of February, so the launch was alongside in
  the early hours of the seventeenth. The notice now reads **entered the seventeenth of February**,
  which also matches the Office's own letter ("consignment received on 17 February").
- **The ending** — the first draft ended by putting Sweeney's name into Sal's ledger. That was wrong
  about the object: the ledger is Sal's book of letters (date, office, subject, COST), not a list of
  people. Replaced with the exchange that keeps the idea and uses the book correctly — *"Write it on
  the back of an envelope. That is where I keep the things for which there is no column."*
