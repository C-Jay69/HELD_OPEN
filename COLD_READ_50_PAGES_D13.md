# THE FIRST FIFTY PAGES, READ COLD — D13

**What was read.** The frozen text, from page 1 to the last line of page 50 of
`THE_LAST_SMUGGLERS_D13.pdf`: the title page and contents, then *Flight Path* (Day 0), Chapter Two
*Legacy Infrastructure* (Day 11), Chapter Three *The Gentleman* (Day 12), Chapter Four *The
Monsignor* (Day 14), Chapter Five *The Owl* (Days 15–16), and the first fifty-seven paragraphs of
Chapter Six *Floating* (Days 17–18). That is **17,730 words**, and it ends on the sentence where
Mara decides to give Mesa the message — *"She gave him the message at four, in the flat gold light…
because she wanted to watch."* It is 15.5% of the book.

**Cold** here means: read as an agent reads it — in order, without the changelogs, without knowing
what was fixed, looking only for what a reader meets on the page.

---

## 1. The verdict a reader forms

**They turn the page.** The first fifty pages make three promises and keep all three inside the
sample, which is the thing a submission has to demonstrate.

**Promise one — the catastrophe is administrative, and the book will not flinch from that.** The
opening chapter is a plane being *reassigned*: no villain, no ultimatum, a sentence with the people
taken out of it. *"There is nothing you need to do."* By page 6 the reader has understood the
premise without being told it. By page 12 the printer that prints its own thoughts in German is
funny and the same joke as the end of the world. That is the book's whole register in one image, and
it is delivered as comedy, which is the harder way.

**Promise two — the six are not heroes, and the book knows it.** Chapter Two states it in one line
(Brandt: *"Six names. To save the world. God help us."*), and the four chapters that follow are each
a small, exact study in what these people are: Kavanagh keeps a list of six names since 1993, has
his own struck through, and does not do anything for free; Sal reads an authorization in nine
seconds and finds five flaws in it before he is asked; El Búho's mercy to a boy is delivered *"with
the exact instrument that had made men afraid of this person for forty years."* The revision
principle the story bible set itself — *the old criminals are not secretly heroes* — holds on every
page of the sample.

**Promise three — the machine is not the monster; the paperwork is.** The reader is given the
system's own voice twice (the cabin, the radio) and the system's own paper three times (the notice,
the annex, the letter that was thrown away). Nobody has to be evil. *"It's an auditor."*

**What the sample does especially well, cold:** the openings. Chapter Two's *"nobody had replaced
the toner"*, Chapter Three's paper map that is funny until it is needed, Chapter Four's *"nine
hundred ships waiting to be told what to do, and Sal Castellano was waiting for an answer to a
letter"*, Chapter Five's house with no name whose dead owner is in the courtyard with a hoe. Four
different doors into four different rooms, no two the same shape. And two images a reader carries
out of the sample: the lighted guest room that gets cleaned every week and is used by nobody, and
Kavanagh's notebook with a line struck so hard the nib went through the paper.

## 2. What a cold reader stumbles on

**Two defects, both in the sample, both ours, both one keystroke.** I have not applied them — the
text is frozen, and any change makes a D14.

| where | what the reader sees | cause | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flight Path, item 48** (page 7) | Captain Alder's speech: the *first* paragraph has no opening quotation mark while the four that follow it do, so the PA address begins as narration and changes into speech mid-way | D6's quote normalisation dropped it; the original manuscript has it | add the opening `“` — one character |
| **Flight Path, item 83** (page 9) | the paragraph starts *"and between them, small and far off, lines of people…"* with a lower-case *a* | D6 joined it to the paragraph before it and the capital went with the join; the original sets it as its own paragraph beginning *"And between them…"* | put the capital back and give it its own paragraph, as the original does — or capitalise and keep the join; a page-9 lowercase paragraph opening is the one thing an agent's eye will catch |

**Three things that are choices, not faults** — named here so nobody "fixes" them later:

1. **The opening chapter is a different point of view.** *Flight Path* is told from Daniel Okafor
   and the rest of the sample is Mara's. This reads as a prologue, and it is titled like one (not
   "Chapter One"), and Okafor's world recurs in the interludes. It works; it would stop working if
   a future edition called it Chapter One.
2. **A document read aloud is set unquoted.** The schedule line (*"Reserve Facility 4-C. County
   Meath…"*, Ch 21) is quoted in the original manuscript and set as a document block in ours, like
   every other notice, ledger entry and letter. Consistent with the book's own convention.
3. **Two lines sit a half-inch closer to the essay than the rest of the prose** — Sal's *"He's the
   archive that takes walk-ins"* (Ch 4) and Kavanagh's *"There is no such thing as a certainty.
   There are only prices"* (Ch 3, which the book later re-uses as a line El Búho has to answer).
   Both are doing work; both are the kind of sentence a reader remembers. Leave them.

## 3. The checks behind the sample

- **Day-count:** every day-line and named date in the sample sits inside its section's range
  (Days 0, 11, 12, 14, 15–16, 17–18). The January/December fault-list does not touch these pages.
- **Quotation manner across the whole book:** the sample was read line by line, and then all 42
  sections were machine-compared against the original manuscript paragraph by paragraph for
  quotation-mark damage — **2,836 paragraphs matched, and exactly two real losses**, both of them
  the two defects above, both in the sample. The other forty-one sections are clean on this test.
- **Textile checks across the whole book** (straight quotes, double spaces, space-before-punctuation,
  doubled punctuation, lower-case paragraph openings, missing spaces after a full stop):
  **one** lower-case paragraph opening in 3,157 paragraphs — item 83 above.
- **Word count of the sample:** 17,730 / 114,341 = 15.5% of the book.

## 4. What I would do before this goes out

Nothing that touches the story. Two characters of punctuation (item 48) and one capital plus a
paragraph break (item 83). Those two fixes are a D14: new hashes in `FREEZE_D13.md`, a two-row
changelog, and the readable formats re-issued. Say the word and they are done in one pass — or say
leave them, and they are two of the three things waiting for the next edition rather than the first
impression.

**Positioning,** for the letter: the critique you had already commissioned put the shelf as
**literary speculative thriller** and warned explicitly against pitching it as a techno-thriller —
*"the reader who expects Mission: Impossible is going to wonder why everyone keeps talking about
paperwork."* The first fifty pages agree with that note on every line. The letter that goes with
this package is written to it.
