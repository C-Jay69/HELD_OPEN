## 0. The verdict, on the second read

I read the revised book end to end, at reading speed, with a pen. Two things happened.

The first: **the first hundred pages are better than I said they were.** I had been reading D3 as an editor, chapter by chapter, looking for the joints. Read straight through, Ch. 1–9 is a genuine recruiting sequence — Kavanagh, Mesa, El Búho, Lin Shu arrive as five different kinds of criminal competence and the reader never once has to be told that these are the only people who can do this. The reader *feels* it. That is the thing that gets a book taken on, and it is already there.

The second: **the middle of the book now behaves.** With the March window closed, Ch. 21–28 reads as one continuous machine — the notice, the plan, the port, the confession, the rules, the plan with a hole in it, the crossing, the register line. Before the fixes there were two places where a careful reader would have had to go back and check a date; now there are none. I re-verified all 37 stamped sections and every calendar reference in the book (see §7), and the only forward-dated items left are the four interstitials, which are documents covering a period and are *placed inside* their own period on purpose.

So the honest answer to "is this submittable?" is: **yes, and the gulf between it and a book that gets signed is not prose — it is four additions, five deletions, and a submission package.** That is what follows, in that order, with locations.

Ceremony — mechanisms walked through a second time, waiting described in order, inventory that no one consults — is what comes out, and ceremony is always in the run-up to a scene, never in the scene.

## 3. What to add

Ranked by what they buy. All four are small; three of them are worth a chapter of anything else in the book, because **a book is taken on the back of its holes, and this book's holes are all in the same place: the women.**

### 3.1 The Ada beat — 150 words, required
Ada Okafor is named **once** in the whole novel: *"Ada. Texas pancakes. NO EXCEPTIONS."*, in Ch. 1, and she never appears again. The man who saves the book (the handwriting that opens the reconciliation, and the reason the ending works) spends the entire book in a building, and the reader is given one line to care about his reason. **This is the cheapest, highest-yield addition available.** One paragraph, in Ch. 34 or the coda, no scene, no reunion: the cost named. The right register is the book's own — an entry, not an emotion. Something in the shape of: *the pancakes were cold, and she ate them anyway, and said they were the best ones, which was the second lie of the new arrangement and the kindest.* (Your sentence, not mine — but that is the size and the temperature.)
**Where:** the coda, in the Okafor entry, which currently ends *"…and it will never be in anybody else's."* One line before that, in the same paragraph, or one standing on its own.

### 3.2 Garrity's bill — 600–900 words, strongly recommended
The critique asked for a character who is "a pain in the arse" and offered Garrity as the vehicle. I disagreed with the remedy in the assessment (§4.4) and still do — but completing this pass has changed my view of the *diagnosis*: the crew's errors cost **other people** (Mesa, and Sal, and the three in Donegal), and never themselves. Garrity's near-death in Ch. 14 is paid for by nobody's authority, and by Ch. 16 he is the man who identifies the beds and by Ch. 26 the man who reads the field. He is owed one cost.
**Where:** Ch. 14–18. The scene is already staged: he flies a machine that cannot do what he promises, and Mara lets it happen because "*not one person in that room was willing to be the one who told the Cowboy he was finished*." Let him do one more of those — an insistence on his own usefulness that quietly costs the crew a thing they needed (a day, an authorization, a route, a witness he frightens off) — and then let nobody say it out loud, because nobody does in this book. 600–900 words, no new characters, no new machinery.
**Why it matters for submission:** it converts the crew from "the criminals who win" into "the people who are wrong about themselves", which is the difference between a good genre novel and one an agent can sell as literature.

### 3.3 Mara's cost — 200–300 words, recommended
Mara is the only character who ends the book having paid nothing personal except a decision she keeps re-stating (*"I asked him to stand where the light was. I will say that in every room until we are finished."*). She says it three times; the book never shows her *losing* something because of it. Either give her one small thing that does not come back — a relationship, an access, a place she can no longer be (she is, by profession, a woman who can go anywhere on a document) — or cut one of the three statements of guilt and let the second silence speak.
**Where:** Ch. 25–29. Cheapest version: in Ch. 29, after the "resolved" letter, one sentence in which something of hers *closes* rather than opens — the annex is being used up, she is a person who now exists in a register too (the Kavanagh receipt shows her the mechanism).

### 3.4 Mesa's last two minutes before the van — 150 words, optional
Mesa's best scene is Ch. 23 and his exit is Ch. 20 — and his invention (**the borrowed cardigan**, a man performing the wrong man) is the single funniest and most moving thing in the book. The hole is that his *decision* to stay is delivered through Father Ward's account (*"I will not be rescued"*), which is beautiful but second-hand. Two hundred words of Mesa alone, before the van, would make the reader complicit rather than informed. Optional because the second-hand telling is also a deliberate strategy and may be the better one.

### 3.5 Not needed, do not add
- **A prologue, a frame, or a flash-forward.** The book opens with the voice on a plane, which is the correct door.
- **Elena's backstory.** She has it twice already (§2.1). Add nothing; subtract.
- **A Sal epilogue.** He is the one character whose fate the book is right to leave in a field with no column.
- **The origin of CUSTOS.** The Office has no head, no founder and no address. That absence is the book's nerve, and one scene of a room where it was built would kill it.
- **Kavanagh's filly.** The assessment suggested denying the crew tidy endings and keeping the September letter as his last word. On a second read I'd keep the racecourse and the fillies: his coda is the book's only comic ending, it is earned by the fifty years he spent not being on a page, and the September letter *is* the ambiguity — an enquiry recorded and closed is exactly what a man who kept the receipt deserves. I have left his entry one clause shorter than it was (the receipt restatement, change log B7).

## 4. Line level — three things left, and the evidence

The 47 edits have removed the retrospective narrator ("*she would think about it for the rest of the book*"), the meta register (six of seven instances), and the worst of the mechanism repetition. What remains is one habit with three faces, and I am reporting it rather than cutting it because it is your ear, not an error.

| Tic | Count | The evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| *"[Name] looked at [thing] for eleven seconds."* | **12** "eleven seconds" in total; at least six in this exact construction (Lin Shu ×3, Crane ×2, Sal, Okafor, El Búho) | Measured across the book, not the page | Keep **two** — Sal's, where the pause is a symptom, and Crane's, where it is the only sign of a frightened man. Vary the other four: some pauses are *long* rather than measured, some are not measured at all. A character who counts seconds twice in a book has a system; a narrator who counts them six times has a tic. |
| *"for four seconds"* | **25** | A pause, a silence, a room doing nothing for four seconds | Consider a rule: four seconds belongs to rooms ("nobody said anything for four seconds"). It should not also belong to individual hesitation. Sweep and vary; this one is cheap and invisible. |
| *"eleven"* | **277** — of which "eleven years" **59**, "eleven minutes" **31**, "eleven days" **28**, "eleven seconds" **12**, "eleven hundred" **10** | The book's unit of institutional time. "Eleven days" is the crisis, the same eleven days in every mouth; "eleven years" is CUSTOS's age and Elena's career and the same eleven years each time | **Do not thin this out.** It is the book's signature and it is doing work: *"The record is the only thing that holds"* is a sentence about repetition, and the prose repeating a number is the argument, not a habit. The only instances I would look at are the decorative ones where eleven is doing the job of "about a dozen" — *eleven feet* (three times, in one chapter), *eleven miles of track*, *eleven vans* (four times in one chapter) — and those are three afternoons of decisions, not a sweep. |

Two more, both one-line decisions, both from the read-through:

1. **Ch. 1 and Ch. 2 open with the same construction** — *"The thing Daniel Okafor would remember afterward was the pasta."* / *"The thing Mara Voss would remember about the end of the world was that nobody had replaced the toner."* Adjacent chapters, same sentence shape, different men. I decided it reads as a signature (two handshake chapters, two registers) and left it. If you disagree, break one of them — never both.
2. **Ch. 25's receipt and Ch. 31's "*You wrote to us on the fifteenth*"** — check the fifteenth against Day 127 (7 April) when you do the additions pass; Sal's letter is posted on 2 April and the Office logs what it receives, so the dates are consistent, but the reader is not meant to be checking this line and you should not have to either.

## 5. Order of work, and roughly what each step costs

1. **Verify the 47 edits** against `CHANGE-LOG.md` in the marked DOCX, rejecting freely. *(One evening. Every rejection has its original string in the log's table.)*
2. **Package** — title page, epigraph decision, filename, word count, name format. *(One hour.)*
3. **The additions** — Ada first (an afternoon), Garrity second (two sessions), then decide on Mara and Mesa. *(Three to four sessions.)*
4. **The five deletions in §2** — in order, top down, and stop at 107k. Do the additions in the same pass so the arithmetic stays honest. *(Two to three sessions.)*
5. **Read Ch. 29–34 aloud** with a pen. Anything you stumble on, cut. This is the safety check on everything above: the ending should *gain* from the cuts in the approach. *(One evening.)*
6. **Line sweep** — the three tics in §4, in one sitting, once. *(One session.)*
7. **Copy-edit** — by a professional, after all of the above. Your tics respond to one deliberate pass; they do not respond to tinkering, and a copy-editor will not do this work for you.
8. **Query package** — one-page synopsis, query letter, three comps, the pitch line. *(I can draft all four; they are the last deliverable in this series and they need your voice, not mine.)*

**Read the Ch. 16 / Ch. 17 / Ch. 19 / Ch. 21 / Ch. 31 endings again before you cut anything.** They are your best chapter endings precisely because they do not explain themselves, and they are the model for what §2 is trying to produce elsewhere.

## 6. Where the last assessment was wrong, and where I've changed my mind

Kept here on purpose, because you asked me to push back and because a second read is allowed to disagree with the first.

1. **The 4,300–5,100-word cut was over-priced.** Read straight through, Ch. 3 and Ch. 6 are not fat — they are five criminal specialisms established at leisure, and the book's middle only works because the reader already trust these people. The revised target is ~3,000–4,000, and I would stop at 107k, not 105k.
2. **Elena is thinner than I thought.** Chapter-by-chapter she looked well-served; in sequence, her two best beats are the same beat (the child who lied, told twice), and her one decisive act — typing *Officer of record: attending in person* — is reported by Roux rather than earned on the page. The de-duplication in §2.1 is not only a trim; it is the fix. If you want a second one, the place to stand is Ch. 32: the chapter is called "Please Clarify Your Objective" and she is the objective — one page of her at the desk, deciding, would land it.
3. **"Kill the meta-book register" was right, and I under-counted it.** I found six instances, not three, plus one I created a trip-up with ("this book" in the coda meaning the novel while "the book" four lines later means Mara's). Both fixed.
4. **The repetition numbers in the original editorial letter were still wrong, and now so are mine from the first pass.** "Eleven" is 277, not 314 (the first count included the contents and every interstitial heading); "record" is 180, "paper" 110. The revised counts are in §4 and the method is in `CHANGE-LOG.md` §5.
5. **"The final 20% is too long" remains wrong**, more strongly than before. Ch. 32–34 is where the book stops being about a machine and becomes about a woman deciding something at 11:52 on a Tuesday morning. Nothing there is ceremony, and the only additions I want in the last fifty pages are Ada's line and one page of Elena.

## 7. What I verified, so you don't have to

- **All 37 stamped sections run forward.** Day 0 → Days 158–189, no inversions. The four apparent ones are the interstitials, which are documents placed inside their own periods (Dakar 18–24 after Ch. 9's Day 23; Notices 78–92 before Ch. 22's 86–93; Kestrel Field Day 89 between Ch. 21 and Ch. 22; Interlude IV Day 143 inside Ch. 32's 142–150).
- **Every calendar reference** in the book reconciles to Day 0 = 1 December: the port interview on the sixteenth, the collection, the movement on 2 April, the van on 4 April, the twenty-first, the sixth of May, the eighth of May, the eighth of June, the coda's June–February.
- **The arithmetic**: 3,841 − 2,961 = 880, and Ch. 33's doorway now says 3,841 so that the difference arrives in Ch. 34.
- **Motif counts** (revised): eleven 277 · forty-one 50 · 211 (ash) 5 · 411 four categories of use · "nothing you need to do" 20 · "the exact voice" 17 · "nineteen minutes" 11 (was 12) · "a man who" 77 · "the way a" 34.
- **No repeated-word typos** ("had had" ×6 are all deliberate speech), no stray chapter numbers, no duplicated paragraphs, and the four interstitials are now numbered I–IV consistently in both the contents and the body.
- **The two "swaps" that would have read as authorial error** are now deliberate: Mesa's intake moved to 18 February so that 2 March is the only March date before the sixteenth, and Kavanagh's receipt now dates to the day he signed.
