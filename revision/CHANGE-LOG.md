## 1. What this pass is, and what it is not

This is the **continuity pass, the line pass, and the three targeted cuts**, applied in one reproducible chain: **939 words of prose removed**, in **66 edits** across the book's 34 chapters, four CUSTOS interstitials and the coda — 41 in the first pass (the fixes and the six line rules), 6 in the second (a full read-through of the result), and 19 in the third (the three targeted cuts in Ch. 11, Ch. 30, and the Coda, removing 633 words of repeated or ceremonial material while leaving the load-bearing recruitment and climax chapters intact).

Every one of the six line rules is applied where the text supports it, and where it doesn't I have said so rather than manufactured a cut — §4 below is the pushback section, and it is longer than usual because three of the six rules turned out to be either misdiagnosed or already satisfied.

**Scope boundary:**
- Instead of a broad 3,000–5,000-word structural cut that would damage the early recruitment chapters, only the three high-value cuts (Elena's duplicated seven-year-old memory in Ch. 11, the pocket/mechanism recital in the opening of Ch. 30, and the four-candidate-hands list in the Coda) have been applied.
- The optional additions (the Ada beat in §3.1 of `SUBMISSION-READINESS.md`, and the Garrity scene in §3.2) are still open for your decision.

**Scope of the file:** the revision contains all 39 sections in D3's order — 34 chapters, the four CUSTOS interstitials (Dakar, Notices, Kestrel Field, Interlude IV) and the coda. Every edit below is anchored to an exact paragraph and verified by exact-string match, so nothing but the listed edits differs — see §5.

### FLAG 2 — why the eighth of March had to move, not be kept

The audit recommended keeping the eighth and making it explicit: *"interviewed at the western facility on the eighth of March."* I tried that, and it breaks the book in a place the audit didn't check.

In **Ch. 22, opening scene (Days 86–93, i.e. 25 February – 4 March)** the crew is already holding that exact letter — not a similar one, the same one: *"Mara had it in front of her — the letter from the office, the one with a typo in it, and she read the middle paragraph out loud for the third time in a week"*, followed by *"The person concerned has requested that his profession be entered as maritime. The request has been granted."* That sentence is verbatim the Ch. 24 letter. A letter written *after* an interview on 8 March cannot be read aloud on 25 February, and the whole Ch. 22 plan (the maritime category → the designated port office) is built on it.

So the March date on that interview is a second relic of the same moved schedule as FLAG 1 — the interview was once the 8th, the appointment moved to the 16th, and this date was left behind. The interview therefore moves to the one part of the window that can hold it: the facility intake, after Mesa's collection (~14 February) and early enough for the letter to reach the crew before Ch. 21's plan meeting. **18 February (Day 79)** sits inside Ch. 21's own Days 77–85 stamp, requires no scene to move, and leaves 2 March as the only March date in the window — which is what Kavanagh's receipt now acknowledges.

Two consequences worth knowing:
1. It also **resolves Housekeeping item 4** (the Ch. 22 "typo" reference being premature). In story time the crew has now had the letter, typo and all, for a week before Ch. 22 opens: *"the one with a typo in it"* stops being a forward reference and becomes a plant that pays off in Ch. 24's *"Wrong word for a man in a room with no door. Accommodated."* No edit was needed in Ch. 22 at all.
2. It makes the blame argument in Ch. 24 a February flashback rather than a March one, which is what the chapter's placement already implies — the divider is already there (the `---` before *"After the interview…"*), so only the date inside the sentence needed to change.

### FLAG 3 and FLAG 5 — the chapter's own span, and the reading order

Ch. 24 is now stamped **Days 105–108** rather than 97–104 (below). Because its frame now begins on the day the collection begins, the printed order (Ch. 23 on the sixteenth, then Ch. 24) is chronological and the "eight days earlier" dateline the audit suggested is unnecessary. Ch. 23 gains the date instead, using the stamp convention the book already uses in Ch. 28 and Ch. 31 ("Day 122 — the second of April").

Reading the revised book end to end, as a reader rather than an editor, turned up three things the first pass could not see — and one of them was created by the first pass.

**The dates are now strictly ordered.** Every one of the 37 stamped sections runs forward from Ch. 1. The three apparent exceptions are the interstitials, and they are deliberate: Dakar (`Days 18–24`) sits after Ch. 9 (`Day 23`), Notices (`Days 78–92`) before Ch. 22 (`Days 86–93`), Interlude IV (`Day 143`) inside Ch. 32's window (`Days 142–150`). Each is a document that covers a period and is placed inside it. That is the book's own system, not a fault, and I have left it alone.

**The two remaining reversed chapters were the last of the March problems.** Ch. 25 opened on Day 105, which Ch. 24 now reaches the end of on Day 108. Moving the stamps (not the scenes) gives 105 | 105–108 | 108–110 | 110–115, and the March chapters stop jumping backwards. FLAG 3's fix is what made this visible: re-stamping Ch. 24 was correct, and it left its neighbour behind — the same failure mode as FLAG 1, one chapter later.

**One date in the coda was a week out.** Okafor's page reached Elena two days before the Ch. 31 interview on the twenty-first of April, not in "the last week of April". In a book whose argument is that a date is the only thing that holds, the last page cannot be the one that rounds.

## 4. Where I disagree with the audit

1. **FLAG 2 — the eighth of March could not be kept.** Full reasoning in §2. This is the only place where the fixes depart from the audit, and it is a departure in the direction of *more* change, not less.

2. **Rule 1 — "the five-line plan is stated in Ch. 26, restated in Ch. 27, and re-explained in Ch. 30" does not hold.** I searched all three chapters for every form of it (the five lines, the four lines, the doors, the objectives, the register/printer mechanism). Ch. 27 contains no restatement of the plan at all — it is the morning of the operation, and what repetition it has is *in-scene planning* (Roux's drill, the five-fifteen start). Ch. 30's material is new: the fire return, the node, the disposition. What actually recurs are the **four doctrine lines**, stated on the board in Ch. 18, again in Ch. 26, and once more as the chapter title of Ch. 28 — and that recurrence is load-bearing, because Ch. 26 tells you why: *"a crew is not held together by hope, it is held together by a format."* I cut one genuine over-explanation (E4, and the near-verbatim repeat at F4). I did **not** cut the doctrine refrain, and I would resist cutting it.

3. **Rule 5 — the similes are voice, not fat.** The audit's number (9.8 per 1,000 in Kestrel Field) put that section at the top; measured consistently across the book it sits mid-pack (2.1), behind Ch. 6 (2.65), Ch. 4 (2.63) and Ch. 3 (2.28) — and in those chapters the similes are almost entirely **character register**: Mesa's auctioneer arithmetic, Kavanagh's horses, Garrity's aircraft. *"set him on deck like a crate of eggs"*, *"the way a man looks at a horse he is considering buying"* — these are the two best characterisations in the book arriving by simile. I cut two narrator-side ones (E1, E2) and left the rest. The rule as written would sand off the book's best asset, which is also what §8 of the assessment warned about.

4. **Rule 4 — the numbers are the book's grammar.** After FLAG 6 (where the slip was real), I made one number cut (E3). Everything else is deliberate: 411 is the Office's own signature count wearing every costume it can — desks, exceptions, entries, aircraft, the June movement, the euro bill — and "eleven" is the book's unit of bureaucratic time. Thinning these is a structural decision, not a line one, and it should be decided with the cut pass, not before it.

5. **The euro receipt (audit housekeeping D3).** The audit was right that the coda's repetition shows the machinery, and I've cut the coda restatement (B7). But I checked Ch. 34 first and left it intact: *"the total, which is on this page in eleven-point type, is four hundred and eleven euro"* is a joke the book has earned, and Kavanagh's *"I paid €411 for a receipt that says I did a thing"* is his whole character in one line. The number is only a problem when the narrator uses it a second time.

## 5. Method, and how to check me

The PDF's text layer gives one line per block, so paragraphing was rebuilt mechanically from line geometry: consecutive lines are joined unless the vertical gap exceeds 1.5× the line pitch (paragraph breaks are exactly twice the line pitch throughout this file). The reconstruction was validated against the D2 manuscript's own paragraphs, chapter by chapter — 73 of 78 paragraph blocks in a sampled chapter were byte-identical, and every mismatch was a scene divider, not prose.

Every edit is then applied by **exact-string match against one numbered paragraph**, with the match required to be unique; if a string had moved or read differently the build fails rather than guessing. The result:

- **66 paragraphs carry edits, 82 carry a normalised divider, 3204 are untouched.** No paragraph was edited that is not listed in §2–§3, and no edit was made in a paragraph that is not listed.
- **Word count, honestly stated.** The 66 edits remove **939 words** of prose. Counting the novel from Chapter One to the last line: **109,882 → 108,939**. As a word processor reports the DOCX it is **109,406**, because the front matter (title page, epigraph, contents) and the 82 scene dividers are counted as well — each divider is three spaced bullet glyphs, which Word counts as three words. The number to quote in a query is **109,000** (or **110,000**).
- Insertions are highlighted and deletions struck through in `D3_REVISED_marked.docx`; the paragraph's surrounding text is untouched in both DOCX files.
- `D3_REVISED_manuscript.md` is the same text, clean, for reading and for searching.

**What to do next, in this order:** read Ch. 23–24 in the clean file first (that's where the surgery was); then the marked file, top to bottom, rejecting freely; then the cut pass in `revision-plan.md`. When you reject an edit, the change-log row gives you the exact original string to paste back.
