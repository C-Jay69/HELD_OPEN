# Revision plan — from D3 (109,782) to a submission draft

The goal is not a number. The goal is that every remaining word is either **consequence**, **comedy**, **character** or **the machine**. Nothing else survives.

**Recommended total:** cut **4,300–5,100 words**, then stop at ~105k. If you want a round number and a shorter book, the second tier below gets you to ~102–103k — and I'd still leave the last three chapters alone.

Why not 17k: you've already taken ~12,000 out (see `EDITORIAL-ASSESSMENT.md` §0 and `data/draft-comparison.csv`). The 8–12k/17k recommendation was priced against a 118k draft. A further 17k would take you to ~93k, and it would have to come from the second half, which is where the book's argument lives.

## The line pass — rules, in order of application

Apply these in a single sweep, after the structure is locked. Each has a measured target.

1. **Numbers carry meaning or they don't exist.** `eleven` should mean: the eleven days, eleven governments, the eleven bulletins, the eleven weeks, eleven exceptions, eleven o'clock. It should not mean eleven centimetres, eleven seconds, eleven minutes, eleven miles, eleven feet (see `data/repetition-density.csv`; densest offenders: Kestrel Field 9.8/1k — defensible; Ch. 2 6.2; Ch. 22 5.6; Ch. 34 5.2). Same for `forty-one` (Ch. 18 alone has eleven) and for `nineteen minutes` / `second and a half`, which have become a verbal habit rather than a system.
2. **The signature simile gets one rule:** keep `a man who…` / `the way a man…` only when the second half of the comparison is *specific and earned* — a trade, a machine, an object, a memory. Cut it when it's a mood ("the way a man looks at a horse he is considering buying" is earned; "the way a man who has decided something looks" is not). Your D4 checklist already isolates these: 76 + 22 + 22 instances.
3. **One explanation per mechanism.** The plan, the schema, the reconciliation and the "completed person" rule are each explained more than once. Keep the first, cut the rest, and never let a character re-explain to a character who already knows.
4. **Cut the paragraph after the scene.** Not all of them — the book's rhythm is scene-then-gloss, and the glosses are often good. But every chapter should be checked for a final paragraph that tells the reader what to feel. The chapters that don't do this (Ch. 15, Ch. 19, Ch. 21, Ch. 31) are your strongest endings.
5. **Reserve the meta register.** One wink maximum ("THE BOOK ABOUT ALL OF THIS WILL NEVER BE WRITTEN" is the one to keep, if you keep one; "whatever the chapter is called in your notebook" and "for the rest of the book" should go).
6. **Exposition by refusal.** Your best explanations are the ones your characters decline to give: "Do not repeat the question, Ms. Voss." Every time someone explains the Office politely and fully, see if the scene is better with "No."

## Pass order (and roughly how long it should take)

1. **Continuity** — the seven flags in `continuity-audit.md`. One working session. No rewrites; six of the seven are one-sentence fixes.
2. **Structure** — Tier 1 map, top of the table downward. Do the additions in the same pass so the arithmetic stays honest.
3. **Read the last three chapters aloud** with a pen. Anything you stumble on, cut. This is also the safety check: the ending should *gain* from the cuts in the approach (Ch. 29/30), because the quiet of Ch. 33/34 will land harder.
4. **Line pass** — the six rules above, one sweep. Do not line-edit before this point; you'll do it twice.
5. **Then** a copy-editor, a title decision on the interlude series, and the query package with the pitch line from §4.6 of the assessment.

## One worked example

**Before (Ch. 22, the arithmetic that breaks the window):**

> "The application went on the ninety-first day. … 'Four days for the category review. Two more to generate the appointment.' … 'The sixteenth. A designated office at the Port of Dublin, at twenty to ten in the morning, which is the most cheerful time of day to do an unreasonable thing.'"

**After:**

> "The application went on the ninety-first day. … 'Eleven days for the category review. Three more to generate the appointment.' … 'The sixteenth. A designated office at the Port of Dublin, at twenty to ten in the morning, which is the most cheerful time of day to do an unreasonable thing.'"

Nothing else changes; the reader never sees the join; the date now reconciles with every other reference in the March window.

**Before (Ch. 24, the summary after the scene):**

> "She should have taken it. She sat there for a full minute with the whole of her authority in the room and the one page in front of her, and she did not take it, and she would spend the rest of the book being unable to decide whether that was the most humane decision she ever made or the worst one, and in the end she settled on the truth, which was that it was the same decision twice."

**After:**

> "She had the whole of her authority in the room and one page in front of her, and she did not take it. She would never decide whether that was the most humane thing she did or the worst; it was the same decision twice."

Same meaning, 45 words instead of 80, no narrator reaching for the reader's collar, and the meta-register ("the rest of the book") gone.
