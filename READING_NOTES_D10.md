## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Four things that are wrong, and are one-line fixes

**(a) Sal Castellano's age is 77 in the file and 79 in his mouth and in the Office's own record.**
Chapter Two's register entry reads `CASTELLANO, SALVATORE — 77 — New York.` Every other age in that
list is exact: Rinaldo 74, Mesa 69, Kavanagh 68, Garrity 71, Lin Shu 63 — each one matches what the
character says or what the narrator says elsewhere. Sal is the only mismatch. He says *"I am
seventy-nine years old, I have no estate and no dependants"* (Ch 13), *"Then I am seventy-nine, and
I am the only man here who would enjoy it"* (Ch 13), *"I am seventy-nine"* (Ch 17), *"I am
seventy-nine years old and my memory is failing in a manner that I do not expect to reverse"*
(Ch 25), and the Office's own assessment in Ch 25 records *"Subject is seventy-nine years of age."*
Seventy-nine is also the number the book's arithmetic needs: he has spent **sixty-four years**
making files move men (Ch 22, 24, 25, 26, 28, 30) and his confraternity was formed in **1961** (Ch
25) — at 79 he began at fifteen, which is exactly the kind of fact this book is made of. At 77 he
began at thirteen. **Fix: `77` → `79` in the Chapter Two register. It is the only place in the book
that says 77.**

**(b) "Nineteen words." counts a one-word answer.** In Chapter Nine, El Búho's reply to the
accusation about Tomás is:

> "Yes," said El Búho.
> Nineteen words. No explanation. No apology.

*Yes* is one word. I checked the base manuscript; it is written that way in the original, so it is
not something an amendment broke — it reads as the survivor of an earlier, longer reply. **Fix:
`Nineteen words.` → `One word.`** (If you would rather keep the longer measure, the sentence that
would have to be nineteen words is the one El Búho gives later — *"He was going to tell them where
the tunnel was…"* — and that one is closer to sixty, so I would not try to make the count land
there.)

**(c) A paragraph of Elena's broadcast has lost its quotation marks.** Chapter Eleven, the Kestrel
Field address. Items around it all carry speech marks — `"The standard being enforced says one
thing…"` (closed), then this paragraph plain, then `"We are now enforcing it exactly. Perfectly.
Without exception."` The plain paragraph is the antecedent of that sentence — *"It has been the law
of the world for a hundred years and it has been enforced approximately never"* — so it is speech,
not narration, and it is the only paragraph in the whole broadcast without marks. **Fix: add the
opening and closing marks**, or run it into the paragraph before it.

**(d) Mesa has known Rinaldo "twenty-nine years" in Chapter Six and "thirty years" in Chapter
Nine — and the rupture is dated 1994 in both.** Mesa's brother Tomás is taken at Ojinaga on the
**fourteenth of March 1994**, and Ch 6 says *"Twenty-nine years. Twenty-nine years, and the man
sends me a message"*; Ch 9 says *"For thirty years I have told people that Rinaldo Villalobos is a
man with no bottom to him"*; Ch 9 also has *"I have not slept properly since 1994."* December 2025
is thirty-one years and nine months after March 1994. Twenty-nine is the outlier, and it is the
first number the reader gets. **Fix (recommended): Ch 6 `Twenty-nine years` → `Thirty-one years`**,
which keeps Ch 9's round "thirty years" in the same neighbourhood and makes the first number the
accurate one. Alternatively make Ch 6 read *"Thirty years"* and leave the rest — but then two
characters are rounding a hard date in a book that never rounds a hard date.

### 1.1b Five more, found in Chapters 12, 13 and 18 — including one that the D6 pass caused and one that D10 caused

**(e) The doctor who lasted eleven minutes, or twelve.** Sal's 1996 assessment is described
three times, and the three do not agree:
- Ch 4: *"A doctor who lasted **eleven** minutes, and a form with three boxes on it."*
- Ch 13: *"A doctor looked at you in 1996 for **twelve** minutes and wrote that you were not fit to be interviewed."*
- Ch 25: *"The doctor who lasted **twelve** minutes and wrote that you were not fit to be interviewed."*

The cause is mine, in D6: the "motif rationing" pass thinned 164 over-used *eleven* phrases and
rationed `eleven minutes` from 30 to 8 — and two of the three instances it changed describe the
**same eleven minutes**. Ch 4 was protected (it is the motif's first occurrence); the other two were
changed. So the book now contradicts itself about a founding fact. **Fix: Ch 13 and Ch 25 back to
`eleven minutes`** — or, if the repetition still grates, cut the duration from one of them
(*"A doctor looked at you in 1996 and wrote that you were not fit to be interviewed"*), which keeps
the rationing and the fact.

**(f) Six weeks that are now two and a half.** Ch 12, Day 33: *"**Six weeks earlier** she had come
with a bus and a story, a woman with a job."* She came to his house on Days 15–16. Day 16 to Day 33
is **seventeen days**. This is a calendar casualty: the day-counting was made exact in D8, and this
sentence — which is in the original manuscript — was never measured against it. **Fix:
`Seventeen days earlier`.** (There is a cluster of these; see §1.4.)

**(g) Kavanagh has been at this forty-one years or sixty.** Ch 12, his own letter: *"I have been in

the best news of the year or the worst, and I have been at this **sixty** years."* He is sixty-eight.
Forty-one years makes him twenty-seven at the start; sixty makes him eight. **Fix: `sixty years` →
`forty years`** (or repeat forty-one, which is the motif).

**(h) Mara met Sal Castellano in New York, but two later chapters say Antwerp.** Ch 4 is the Port of
New York and New Jersey — the Bayonne Bridge, the Kill, St Anselm's, the porch. But Ch 13 has
*"you told me in **Antwerp** that you remember all of them"*, and Ch 25 has *"for the first time
since the porch in **Antwerp** in December he looked frightened"*. The slip is in the original
manuscript (and a third instance — *"Mara had spent a day in his company in Antwerp"* — was already
removed by an earlier pass). Antwerp is where Sal's *life* is: the house, the letter books under the
bed, berth forty-one, the Medical Board, the standing order. It is not where they met. **Fix:

**(i) My own D10 edit broke a pair of sentences in Chapter 18.** I changed *"which was nine weeks to
the day"* to **ten** so the withdrawal would not land on 21 April, the day of the Elena interview.

of the run: *"**In about nine weeks** there will be a notice on a wall in a port office that says the
vessel is withdrawn."* The narration that answers it is the line I changed — *"exactly what Mara had
said she did not want."* **Fix: `In about nine weeks` → `In about ten weeks`**, which keeps my
reason for changing it and puts the pair back in agreement. (Reverting my D10 edit would also work,
but ten weeks keeps the collision with 21 April out of the book, which was the point.)

### 1.1c The big one: the door, told twice with different facts (Chs 9, 15, 33)

This is the largest thing in the read, and it is not a word — it is the same scene in two versions.

**Ch 9 (Roll Call, Day 23), Mesa to the table:**
> *"Tomás Esquivel Mesa. My brother. Thirty-one. He was in the tunnel at **Ojinaga** on the **fourteenth of
> March** with forty others, and you were **three hundred metres away in a house with a radio**. They took
> him. You knew they would take him. And you closed the door."* — and later *"You came to the funeral."*
> *"I stood at the back."* And Mesa: *"I have not slept properly since 1994."*

**Ch 15 (The Railroad Runs Both Ways, Days 44–47), Lin Shu extracting it:**
> *"**Tomás Iriarte**. **Morelia**. **November 1998**. Tell me about the door."* — and El Búho:
> *"There is a tunnel under **that house**. Four hundred metres, to a drainage cut… if I had opened the
> hatch he would have been up in the middle room in nine seconds and out through the courtyard and over
> the wall, and there would have been six men in **my house asking my wife** questions, and **my daughter**
> in the front room… I closed the hatch… **Eleven days later** he was found in a field outside Apatzingán.
> I went to the funeral. I stood at the back."*

The reconciliation is in Ch 33, where El Búho recites the nine and says *"Tomás Esquivel Mesa, **called
Tomás Iriarte in Morelia**, taken from the far end of the cut and found in a field **twelve days** later."*
So the two names are one man — that part is deliberate and it works. What does not work:

| | Ch 9 | Ch 15 | Ch 12 / Ch 33 |
|---|---|---|---|
| place | Ojinaga | the house outside Morelia | Morelia (Ch 33) |
| date | 14 March **1994** | **November 1998** | 1994 implied ("since 1994") |
| El Búho's position | 300 m away, in a house with a radio | at the hatch of his own house | — |
| found after | — | **eleven** days | **twelve** days (Ch 33) |
| his wife | — | alive, in the house | **died in 1996** (Ch 12, 16, 33) |

Three of those cannot all be true. The wife settles the date: she is in the house, so the door is
**before 1996**, which makes Ch 9's 14 March 1994 right and Ch 15's November 1998 wrong. The house
settles the place: Ch 12 gives that house a middle room, a hatch, a stair and a four-hundred-metre
tunnel to the arroyo; Ch 15 and Ch 33 put the door there, so Ch 9's "Ojinaga" is the outlier. And the
"eleven days" of Ch 15 and the "twelve days" of Ch 33 are the same interval — eleven is the original
(it is also the motif), twelve is another fingerprint of the D6 rationing.

**Fix (three words and one clause):** Ch 15 `Morelia. November 1998.` → `Morelia. March 1994.` ·
Ch 9 `in the tunnel at Ojinaga` → `in the tunnel under your own house`, and `you were three hundred
metres away in a house with a radio` → something the staging can carry (e.g. *"and you were upstairs
with your hand on the hatch"*) · Ch 33 `twelve days later` → `eleven days later`.

### 1.1i The last chapters: four more numbers, and one date that cannot be two dates

**(r) The category review has a clock that does not reach the sixteenth.** Chapter 22, Kavanagh,

move the application to the ninety-first day — but the ninety-first day is the day Mesa is

The second of March is the date of the other letter in the same week (Mesa's interview, Ch 25), and
it has been copied across. **Fix: `the second of March` → `the eighth of March`.**

**(t) The man Sal resolved is resolved on the ninth, the eleventh and the twelfth.** Three chapters

The base manuscript had the eleventh in both places, so the split is ours (the D6 ration moved
"the eleventh" in eight places; two of them were these). The ninth is the one the letters support.

**(u) Mesa's ninety.** Chapter 24, entering the eighty-one: *"the number is ninety now, because of
the four"*. Eighty-one plus four is **eighty-five**. **Fix: `ninety` → `eighty-five`.**

**(v) "the fat man" is a man the book never introduces.** Chapter 27, Garrity refusing to fly again:
*"I did that in December and it cost us a wrist and a leg and it very nearly cost the **fat man's**

### 1.5 The envelope — one letter, three statements about it

Chapter 12, El Búho hands Lin Shu an envelope and asks her to carry it:
> *"a small envelope, gone soft at the corner, with a name and an address on it in a hand that was not
> his. 'A woman in Rotterdam.'"* … *"You will put it in her hand."*

in her hand without having to look at her face… Ask her yourself."*

> *"you will read to us what **your wife wrote to your daughter** in 1996."* — and El Búho: *"it is not
> addressed to you. It is addressed **to her**."*

> *"Mara saw her read it, the way anyone reads their own handwriting from when they were eleven: all
> at once, from the outside."* — Elena: *"**I handed it to you in a corridor**, and you put it in your
> coat, and you have not opened it."*

Rotterdam. Three chapters, one envelope. Ch 34 is the scene, and it is unanswerable, so the other two
are the ones to change: **Ch 26 `what your wife wrote to your daughter` → `what your daughter wrote
in that corridor`**, and **Ch 12 `A woman in Rotterdam.` → something the room can hold** (*"A woman
who has not spoken to me in twelve years."* — which also makes Lin Shu's answer land).

pocket since the day he left Ireland. **Fix: drop the comparison** — *"in the same sloped hand a child
uses when it is being careful"* — and the bag holds its own letter.

### 1.6 The one count I could not reconcile: El Búho's refusals
