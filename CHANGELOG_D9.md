## 1. Daniel Okafor now appears three times in the middle instead of once

He opened the book on page one and then was absent for a hundred pages: five of forty sections.
He is now in seven, and the three middle appearances are short, in his own close third, and add no
plot — they only make the reader wait for the handwriting they already know is coming.

**Beat A — end of *Chapter Eleven — The Prophet* (Day 30, 505 words).**
Elena's chapter ends with her on the roof over two hundred and eleven grounded aeroplanes. The
chapter now ends with the same day seen from inside Hangar 4: four hundred and eleven records at
eight, closed by six, the fourth question on the front end *his*, the broadcast coming through the
same speakers as the fire instruction, nobody stopping work — *"there is no field in a front end
for the sound of a thing."* Then he asks the one question he ever asked about the machine, in a
cold corridor, of the woman in the good coat coming down off the roof: *"The expedited offer. In
the schedule. Four days instead of forty. Who is it for?"* She looks at him for a second and a
half, says *"It's cold in this corridor,"* and goes down the stairs. (This is the corridor question
Interlude III already refers to in summary; the reader now sees it happen thirty chapters earlier
than the summary, and the summary reads as memory rather than information.)

**Beat B — end of *Chapter Twenty-Two — Ransom, or Something Like It* (Day 96, 393 words).**
The chapter still closes on the CUSTOS bulletin about handwritten pages. That bulletin is now read
by the man it is about. He started keeping the book *the day before* — two lines in it, two
reference numbers, records he closed to standard and did not believe — and the office, which has
never in eleven years done anything without a form, is announcing that ink is accepted, scanned,
dated and entered, and not answered. He reads the last line, the line every bulletin in the
building ends with, which he has read four hundred times without hearing: *There is nothing you
need to do.* Then: *"he did not write anything in the book that day, or for twenty-six days."*
(Twenty-six days from Day 96 is **Day 122 — the second of April, Chapter Twenty-Nine — Four Doors**,
where he writes the two numbers. The arithmetic is exact.)

**Day 122 he already had.** Four Doors was always his third appearance; nothing about it changed.

## 2. The two climax chapters are broken at their natural turns (no words changed)

| was | is now | words |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 31 — The Core | **Ch 31 — The Four Sentences** (Days 133–140) · **Ch 32 — The Core** (Days 140–141) | 2,001 + 1,850 |
| Ch 32 — Father | **Ch 33 — Father** (Day 141) · **Ch 34 — Off the Record** (Day 141, the same morning) | 3,347 + 866 |

*The Core* was 3,851 words in **one unbroken scene — ninety-one paragraphs with no section break in
it**. The seam is item 45, *"On the night of the twentieth they went down to the quay"* — the point
where the plan stops being an argument in a house and becomes a room in a port. The first half is
named for the engine of it: the letter of four sentences, and the three lines on a page with a
fourth crossed out. The second half keeps the old title, because the second half is where they
actually reach the core.

*Father* now breaks where the recorder goes off: *"Then she stood up, and the room got quiet in the
way a room gets quiet when the thing that has been listening stops."* Everything after it is off
the record — the envelope, *"Take it back"* — and it is now a short final chapter of 866 words
instead of the tail of a 4,213-word one. Nothing was moved, cut or added; the section break that
stood there was replaced by a chapter break.

Recovery of numbering: chapters **31–37** are renumbered; the interludes keep I–IV. The contents
list has 42 sections.

## 3. The Office's letter was being read twice — fixed

**The defect:** in *Chapter Twenty-Two*, Mara *"read the middle paragraph out loud for the third
time in a week"* from *"the letter from the office, the one with a typo in it"* — and then in
*Chapter Twenty-Five*, the same letter arrived for the first time and **Garrity discovered the
typo.** A reader who noticed would have lost the discovery, and a reader who did not would have
lost confidence.

**Three sentences changed:**

| where | was | is now |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 22 | "the letter from the office, the one with a typo in it" | "the letter that had come from the office on **the fourth of March**, forwarded by the man who signs for everything" |
| Ch 25 | "After the interview at the western facility on the second of March, the Office wrote to them." | "The letter about the interview at the western facility **had come on the fourth of March and had been read in the kitchen the evening it arrived, and twice more that week**, and it was in the room on the seventh when they worked out what the word maritime was worth." |
| Ch 25 | "Roux opened it in the filing hall at four in the afternoon and read it out to five people…" | "**On the sixteenth, with the house full of paper and nothing else to do, Roux read it out again** in the filing hall, slowly, from the top, to five people who had spent a fortnight not looking at one line in the middle of it and were now going to have to." |

The typo is now Garrity's alone, and the fourth reading of the letter is an act — Roux making a
room look at the sentence it has been avoiding — instead of an arrival.
